# SwasthyaQueue Demo Case Studies

This sheet is meant for demos, presentations, and explaining why OPD digitization matters.

Each case can be shown end to end:

- patient / reception entry
- nurse triage
- doctor consultation
- visible outcome

## 1. General Medicine

### Scenario

A returning patient has fever, weakness, and body pain.

### Traditional pain point

- patient repeats full details again
- no easy view of previous visit
- queue visibility is poor

### SwasthyaQueue demo flow

1. Patient logs in by OTP.
2. Books a new visit with symptoms.
3. Token is generated.
4. Nurse records vitals.
5. Doctor reviews symptoms + vitals + past history.
6. Doctor records diagnosis and prescription.
7. Visit appears in history.

### Why it matters

- returning patients do not start from zero
- queue is transparent
- doctor has more context

## 2. Cardiology

### Scenario

A patient reports chest discomfort and dizziness.

### Traditional pain point

- urgent patient may get delayed in the general line
- no structured nurse escalation

### SwasthyaQueue demo flow

1. Reception does quick intake.
2. Patient is immediately added to queue.
3. Reception flags urgent review.
4. Nurse checks vitals and marks higher priority.
5. Doctor sees patient in consultation list with clinical context.

### Why it matters

- urgent cases are surfaced sooner
- nurse escalation becomes visible and tracked

## 3. Orthopedics

### Scenario

A walk-in patient with knee pain and swelling does not have a phone.

### Traditional pain point

- desk struggles to identify or track patient later
- no digital record exists

### SwasthyaQueue demo flow

1. Reception creates staff-assisted account.
2. Internal assisted reference is generated.
3. Patient enters queue.
4. Nurse records vitals / note.
5. Doctor records diagnosis, advice, and follow-up note.

### Why it matters

- even no-phone patients become trackable
- front desk and doctor share the same record

## 4. Pediatrics

### Scenario

A parent brings a child with fever and cough.

### Traditional pain point

- parent has to repeat child details again
- future follow-up tracking is weak

### SwasthyaQueue demo flow

1. Reception creates or finds child record.
2. Visit is booked.
3. Nurse triage records vitals.
4. Doctor completes consultation.
5. Child visit stays in history.

### Future extension

Family member / dependent linking can make this much stronger:

- parent verified as primary user
- child linked as dependent
- parent books and tracks pediatric visits

## 5. Emergency

### Scenario

A patient arrives in distress and cannot complete registration first.

### Traditional pain point

- paperwork blocks urgent handling
- data is written later and may be missed

### SwasthyaQueue demo flow

1. Reception uses quick intake.
2. Patient is placed in queue immediately.
3. Nurse triages and escalates.
4. Doctor sees ready patient quickly.
5. Profile completion can happen later.

### Why it matters

- queue entry first, paperwork later
- supports real rush-case behavior

## 6. ABHA-Linked Future Case

### Scenario

A patient arrives with ABHA-linked records available.

### Future flow

1. Patient verifies ABHA identity.
2. Hospital links local account after consent.
3. Visit is created without re-entering everything.
4. Doctor sees better continuity across visits.

### Demo talking point

This shows how SwasthyaQueue can move from “digital token system” to “digital OPD continuity layer.”

## 7. Family Member Future Case

### Scenario

A daughter manages care for her elderly parent.

### Future flow

1. Primary account is verified.
2. Parent is added as family member / dependent.
3. Daughter books visit for parent.
4. Reception, nurse, and doctor still work on the same patient record.

### Demo talking point

This is important for real-life Indian family care patterns, where one person often manages visits for another.

## Presentation Message

When presenting this system, the strongest message is:

SwasthyaQueue is not only a digital queue board.

It is a step toward:

- better reception management
- structured nurse triage
- cleaner doctor handoff
- patient-visible visit history
- future ABHA and family-linked healthcare workflows
